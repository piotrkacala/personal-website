400m

GPX Track Laps

Estimate track laps and corrected distance from GPX files.

400m is a local-first web app for runners on standard athletics tracks. Upload a GPX file, choose the lane explicitly, and calculate corrected distance from lap progress instead of raw GPS path length.

Why it exists

GPS watches often overestimate distance on 400 m tracks because repeated tight curves create drift and signal jitter. This tool estimates completed laps plus explicitly estimated partial progress, then uses the selected lane length for the result.

How distance is calculated

Corrected distance is always derived from total laps multiplied by the selected lane length. The app does not silently infer or override the lane. Raw GPX distance remains a diagnostic value only.

Privacy

GPX analysis runs entirely in the browser. No backend is required, no GPX file is uploaded to a server, and no telemetry is needed to compute the result.

Track measurement

Lane 1 is 400.00 m. Outer lanes use official measurement-line geometry, so lane 6 is 437.70 m per lap rather than a rounded 438 m.

FAQ

How should the corrected result be interpreted?

Corrected distance is estimated total_laps multiplied by the tabulated length of the lane selected by the user. Completed-lap topology has stronger evidence than within-lap position, so progress after the last completed lap is explicitly an angular estimate.

What do controlled tests show?

Clean synthetic 10-lap lane-1 and lane-6 cases had effectively zero lap-count and distance error. A lane-8 case with deterministic 3 m coordinate noise had 0.00928-lap error, equivalent to 4.202 m. A separate noisy fixture recovered all 20 reference half-lap boundaries inside the 10 m gate. These synthetic fixtures are evidence, not a universal guarantee for real recordings.

What do real files show?

Three real lane-6 recordings from one runner protect regression behavior. Their lap totals and crossing times were verified manually, and the analysis results agree with them.

Why are there no exact 200 m or 100 m splits?

Intentional direction changes after completed laps are supported. The product omits exact 200 m, 100 m, and straight-only timing. It may show estimated pace for two halves of an eligible completed lap; in outer lanes each half is longer than 200 m.

When is the result weaker?

Missing or invalid timestamps disable timing. Long gaps, noisy or sparse traces, partial laps, discontinuous segments, and a dominant dense cluster away from the track weaken interpretation. The 5 km markers have a known outer-lane within-lap limitation.

Is the GPX file uploaded?

No. The hosted web app downloads its own assets from the host, but the selected GPX file is parsed and analyzed in the browser. Coordinates, timestamps, and analysis results are not sent to an application backend.
